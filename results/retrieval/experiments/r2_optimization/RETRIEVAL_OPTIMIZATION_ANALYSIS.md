# R2: query construction, hybrid retrieval and diversity re-ranking

_Generated by `scripts/r2_05_write_docs.py` from the R2 artifacts. Retrieval-validation queries only (the R1 primary paired set); the locked retrieval-test split was not opened; the classifier was not modified; RAG was not started._

## 1. Objective

R1 showed that oracle finding queries retrieve well while queries from the frozen classifier lose about 0.29 Jaccard@3. R2 asks whether better query construction (phrase expansion, confidence weighting, Top-N finding selection), dense-lexical fusion and diversity re-ranking can narrow that gap, and fixes a provisional Top-K for RAG.

## 2. Population, protocol and R1 reproduction

All comparisons use the exact R1 primary paired validation set (358 studies); nothing was re-split, removed or redefined. The selection protocol (`r2_selection_protocol.json`) and the fixed clinical expansion mapping (`finding_query_expansion.json`) were written before any R2 evaluation; the protocol's replacement rule is: a challenger replaces the incumbent only if the lower bound of the paired bootstrap 95% CI of the Jaccard@3 difference is above zero (1000 resamples, seed 42, queries as the unit, one shared set of resampling indices).

R1 baselines (oracle and classifier all-positive / gated queries, dense and BM25) were reproduced exactly with the R1 ranking order: 2,238 query rows, 0 differing lists, maximum metric difference 1.1e-16. R2 uses one deterministic tie rule (score descending, then corpus order) because R1's FAISS order for exactly tied reports depends on the search depth; this reorders exact ties in 599 of the 2,238 lists and leaves every primary-set mean Jaccard@3 unchanged (largest absolute difference 0.0e+00).

## 3. No Finding query phrase

Candidates `no acute abnormality`, `normal chest radiograph`, `no acute cardiopulmonary abnormality` were compared on the 207 eligible validation studies whose true finding set is {No Finding} (oracle-normal; dense retrieval). Jaccard@3 was no acute abnormality 1.000; normal chest radiograph 1.000; no acute cardiopulmonary abnormality 0.667. Because every normal query uses one fixed phrase, all normal studies share one retrieved list and the paired interval is degenerate; the rule reduces to 'strictly better than the R1 phrase'. No candidate was better, so `no acute abnormality` is kept (the R1 phrase).

## 4. Stage A: query representation

**Q1, clinical phrase expansion** (one fixed mapping, 13 short phrases; mean query length 5.34 words against 2.98): Jaccard@3 0.503 (95% CI 0.455 to 0.548) versus 0.514 (95% CI 0.465 to 0.560) for Q0, a paired difference of -0.012 (95% CI -0.024 to +0.001). Expansion did not help and, if anything, lowered coverage@3 (0.611 versus 0.646); Q0 is kept.

**Q2, calibrated-probability weighting** (weighted sum of per-finding phrase embeddings, then L2 normalisation; uniform, probability, and margin-above-operating-point weights): paired Jaccard@3 differences against Q0 were -0.000 (95% CI -0.010 to +0.010) (uniform), +0.005 (95% CI -0.006 to +0.018) (probability) and +0.009 (95% CI -0.003 to +0.022) (margin); none is reliably above zero, so weighting is rejected. The simple form max(p - 0.5, 0) was rejected by design: 74.0% of the frozen positive findings have p < 0.5 (the calibrated-equivalent thresholds are far below 0.5), so it would give zero weight to most findings. Weights use the frozen C5 probabilities only and never change a binary decision.

**Q3, Top-N findings** (positives ordered by calibrated probability, never padded): Jaccard@3 differences against all positives were +0.000 (95% CI -0.013 to +0.016) (N=1), -0.002 (95% CI -0.008 to +0.005) (N=2) and +0.003 (95% CI +0.000 to +0.007) (N=3). N=1 lowers coverage@3 (0.625 versus 0.646 for all positives), so truncating the query to one finding loses true findings; adding findings beyond three does not measurably harm retrieval. By the protocol rule N=3 is selected, but its gain is small (it changes only studies with more than three positive findings) and is not practically meaningful on its own.

## 6. Stage C: diversity re-ranking (MMR)

MMR re-ranks the top-30 hybrid candidates (relevance = min-max-normalised RRF score; similarity = cosine of report embeddings; lambda in [0.5, 0.7, 0.9]). MMR was not adopted: no lambda reliably reduced the exact-duplicate rate at both K = 3 and K = 5 (`r2_mmr_adoption_checks.csv`): the duplicate-text rate changed by -0.0067, -0.0022 and -0.0022 at K=5 and by at most 0.0019 at K=3. Relevance did not collapse: at K=3 lambda = 0.7 and 0.9 raised Jaccard@3 by +0.019 and +0.022 and coverage@3 by +0.071 and +0.073, but these gains disappear at K=5 (coverage +0.008 and +0.006). Because the corpus is dominated by near-identical templated reports (mean pairwise cosine among retrieved reports about 0.83), the similarity term cannot separate exact duplicates from template-similar reports, which is a plausible reason why exact duplicates are not removed. The K=3 relevance gain is an exploratory observation that the protocol does not adopt; it has no effect at the provisional K=5.

## 7. Top-K selection

Candidates K = 1, 3, 5, 10 were judged with the declared rule (coverage gain with CI lower bound above zero and at least 0.05, Jaccard@K falling by at most 0.03, duplicate-text rate at most 0.50 with more distinct templates, context at most 1,000 words). Starting at K=1, the rule advanced to K=3 (coverage gain +0.065 (95% CI +0.045 to +0.090)), then to K=5 (gain +0.101 (95% CI +0.071 to +0.129)) and stopped there: K=10 added a coverage gain of 0.044 over K=5 (95% CI 0.026 to 0.064), below the declared 0.05 minimum. At K=5 the context is 148 words on average (K=3: 88; K=10: 313), the duplicate-text rate is 0.404 with 2.98 distinct report texts, and 34.4% of retrieved reports share no finding with the study. Coverage@5 is 0.748, which exceeds the random-retrieval expectation of 0.631 (coverage rises with K even for random reports, so the lift over random is the informative quantity). K = 5 is therefore provisional; it was chosen before the retrieval test split was opened and K=3 is not retained merely because it was planned.

## 8. Oracle-to-classifier gap after R2

The selected pipeline improves Jaccard@3 over the R1 classifier baseline by +0.029 (95% CI +0.019 to +0.042) (dense Q0 0.514 to 0.544) and coverage@5 by +0.072 (95% CI +0.037 to +0.106). The gap to the oracle dense reference narrows from +0.291 (95% CI +0.253 to +0.329) (R1) to +0.261 (95% CI +0.222 to +0.299) in Jaccard@3, and is +0.252 (95% CI +0.218 to +0.286) at K=5. Applying the same pipeline to oracle queries gives Jaccard@3 0.819 (gap to the classifier-query pipeline +0.276 (95% CI +0.238 to +0.316)), so most of the remaining gap is not removable by retrieval changes.

## 9. Sensitivity to exact-text duplicates

Excluding the 100 primary queries whose own report text has an exact copy in the corpus (258 remain) lowers absolute values but keeps the method ordering: the Spearman correlation of the Jaccard@3 ranking of 12 configurations between the full and the reduced set is 0.895, and hybrid remains above dense (0.443 versus 0.412). The official primary results are unchanged.

## 12. Limitations

- Query policy, retriever, MMR setting and K were selected and evaluated on the same retrieval-validation queries; the paired intervals do not account for this selection, so absolute gains are optimistic. The locked retrieval test is the independent check.
- Most R2 differences are small relative to the interval widths; the only practically meaningful improvement is hybrid fusion over dense retrieval; the Top-3 query rule passed the protocol test with a negligible gain.
- The oracle-to-classifier gap remains large; it is driven by the frozen classifier's query errors, which R2 deliberately does not address.
- IU truth comes from MeSH mapped to 14 classes (some studies unusable, Enlarged Cardiomediastinum unevaluable, few positives for several classes); the primary set is about half normal studies; templated reports are common.
- The Top-K rule constants (0.05, 0.03, 0.50, 1,000 words) are declared design choices; coverage at larger K rises partly by chance, as the random reference shows.
- MMR was evaluated with one similarity definition and three lambdas; no cross-encoder or learned re-ranker was tried.
- RRF used the standard k = 60 and depth 100 without tuning; BM25 was used on short finding-name queries.

## 13. R2 conclusion

The best-supported classifier-query configuration is finding names (top-3 positives) with hybrid RRF retrieval and K = 5: Jaccard@3 0.544, Jaccard@5 0.557, coverage@5 0.748. Phrase expansion, probability weighting and MMR gave no adopted improvement; hybrid fusion improved on dense retrieval. The oracle gap narrowed from 0.291 to 0.261 but remains dominated by upstream classification and query errors. The retrieval test split remains locked and R3 has not been started.
