# Sampling analysis (CheXpert training partition, frontal images)

_Generated by `scripts/eda_03_chexpert_splits.py` from the actual training partition (n = 133,649 frontal images). Positives = label == 1; uncertain and blank are not positive._

## Why multi-label sampling is not a free lunch

A sampler draws **images**, not labels. Upweighting an image because it carries a rare label also upweights every other label on that image. Because rare findings co-occur with common ones (see `chexpert_cooccurrence.csv`), rare-label oversampling also inflates common labels, and the *effective* label distribution seen by the loss changes in ways that per-label reasoning does not predict.

## Schemes evaluated (expected effect, no training performed)

* `inverse_prevalence_max` - weight = 1 / prevalence of the image's rarest positive label
* `inverse_prevalence_mean` - mean inverse prevalence over the image's positive labels
* `inverse_prevalence_sqrt_max` - square root of the max scheme (tempered)
* images with no positive label form their own group weighted by its inverse prevalence

## Evidence-based recommendation

* Under `inverse_prevalence_max`, the four rarest labels (Pleural Other, Pneumonia, Lung Lesion, Fracture) are inflated by 2.5x-7.0x, while the four most common (Edema, Pleural Effusion, Lung Opacity, Support Devices) change by 0.76x-0.87x.
* The same scheme reduces the Kish effective sample size to 46.6% of N, and the top 1% of images carry 7.0% of the sampling mass (repeated exposure of few images -> overfitting risk).
* The largest shift in any P(B | A) is 0.130 (scheme `inverse_prevalence_mean`).

_Decision rule (stated before reading the numbers): a sampler is considered costly if it reduces Kish ESS below 50% of N or shifts any P(B | A) by more than 0.10._

**Recommendation.** The rule is met, so do not enable a sampler in the primary experiments. Loss-level mechanisms (positive-weighted BCE, focal, asymmetric loss) act per label and leave the image distribution, co-occurrence structure and effective sample size intact, so they should be compared first. The tempered `inverse_prevalence_sqrt_max` sampler is the only variant worth keeping as an optional later ablation, and only if loss-level methods leave rare labels under-served.

Validation and test data are never resampled.
