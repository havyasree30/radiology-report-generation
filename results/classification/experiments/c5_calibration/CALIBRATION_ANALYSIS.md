# C5: probability calibration analysis

_Generated by `scripts/c5_write_docs.py` from the stored C5 artifacts. Validation data only: the locked test split was not opened, no model was trained and no inference was rerun._

## 1. Objective

A sigmoid output ranks cases; it is not automatically a probability. A displayed value of 70% is only meaningful if, among images scored near 70%, about 70% are positive. Class weighting during training (√-weighted BCE) deliberately inflates rare-class scores, so miscalibration is expected. C5 quantifies it and fits a per-class calibrator for the **displayed probability**. The **binary finding decision stays with the C4 policy** (raw score, F1-optimal threshold, No Finding rule); calibration does not replace it.

## 2. Inputs and provenance

- Model: DenseNet-121, √-weighted BCE (C2-B, provisional); checkpoint `results/classification/experiments/c2b_sqrt_weighted_bce/checkpoints/best.pt`, SHA-256 `6645ed44be7cb7d4cf4679b1fd561c7ac36be63f964ea320666199883126794b`.
- Data: 28,671 validation images from 9,530 patients, 14 observations in the fixed project order; uncertain labels excluded, blank labels scored negative.
- C4 reproduction: image/patient counts, class order, bit-identical scores, F1 thresholds, raw and final predictions and the No Finding rule all reproduce; macro metric difference 0.0e+00 (True). The rule suppresses No Finding when any of the 12 pathology findings is positive; Support Devices does not.
- Logits: not stored. The stored scores are float32 sigmoid outputs (True), so logit(p) = log(p/(1−p)) is recovered with a worst-case rounding error of 7.8e-05 (median 5.3e-08); no score reached the clipping bounds (0 clipped; score range 3.1e-07–0.9996). No inference was rerun.

## 3. Calibration methods

All calibrators are fitted per class on logits of the valid (non-uncertain) entries.

- **Raw:** the sigmoid score unchanged (baseline).
- **Temperature scaling:** p = σ(z/T), T > 0, fitted on log T by bounded minimisation of log loss. One parameter; it can sharpen or soften but cannot correct a prevalence offset.
- **Platt scaling:** p = σ(a·z + b), fitted by Newton's method on the log loss; a slope ≤ 0 is flagged as pathological.
- **Isotonic regression (exploratory):** non-decreasing piecewise-linear map, outputs clipped to [1e-6, 1 − 1e-6] so log loss stays finite. It has many degrees of freedom and plateaus.

Metrics: Brier score, log loss (primary), expected calibration error with 10 equal-frequency bins (supplementary: it depends on the binning), mean prediction vs prevalence, calibration slope (logistic regression of the outcome on logit(p)) and calibration-in-the-large (offset at slope 1). Ranking metrics (AUROC, AUPRC) are not used to choose a method.

## 4. Patient-level cross-validation

5-fold cross-validation (seed 42) assigns whole patients to folds using multi-label iterative stratification over patients (valid positive images per class). No patient appears in two folds (checked: 0 overlaps). Images per fold: 5,748, 5,730, 5,731, 5,731, 5,731; the smallest per-fold positive count is 76 (Pleural Other). For every method, the calibrator is fitted on four folds and applied to the held-out fold; all metrics below come from the pooled out-of-fold predictions.

## 7. Selected final calibrators

Selection rule (declared before any result): among calibrators with no pathological fit and no C4-decision mismatch, take the simplest one within 0.5% of the best on **both** Brier and log loss; if one method is chosen for at least 10 classes and using it everywhere costs at most 1% in every class, use it uniformly.

- Per-class choices: {'platt': 12, 'raw': 1, 'temperature': 1}. Platt was chosen for 12 classes; raw (Lung Opacity) and temperature (Pleural Effusion) were chosen for the others only because they came within the tolerance of Platt there.
- Applying Platt uniformly costs at most 0.29% in any class, so **Platt scaling is used for all 14 classes** (`final_calibrators.json`).
- Isotonic was eligible but not chosen: it is not better than Platt on the primary metrics, it only provides a non-decreasing map (plateaus), and it has far more parameters. Its lower ECE is supplementary evidence.
- Guardrails: no calibrator was flagged (no non-positive or extreme Platt slope, no temperature at the search boundary, no isotonic fit with fewer than 5 output levels). Platt slopes across folds vary by at most 0.043 within a class.

## 10. Limitations

- Calibration was developed and evaluated on validation data (out-of-fold, so each evaluated image was not used to fit its own calibrator). The final calibrators were then fitted on all validation data and have no further independent evaluation.
- The locked test split has not been evaluated; calibration on it is the next independent check.
- Rare classes have limited positives (smallest per-fold count 76); their calibration curves rest on few events per bin.
- Calibration can drift under dataset shift (different hospitals, scanners, label sources).
- A calibrated probability here means P(label positive | image) under the project's label definition (uncertain excluded, blank scored negative, report-derived labels). It is not the probability that the finding is truly present.
- Calibration is per class; it does not guarantee joint calibration across the 14 outputs.
- The loss was chosen provisionally; a different final model requires refitting the calibrators.
- The effect on report generation has not been tested.

## 11. Decision

Carry **Platt-calibrated probabilities** (`final_calibrators.json`, one calibrator per class, fitted on the full validation set) to the final pipeline as the displayed probabilities. Keep the C4 policy for binary calls; use the calibrated-equivalent thresholds only to show where each call falls on the probability scale. Raw sigmoid scores remain model scores and must not be shown as probabilities.
