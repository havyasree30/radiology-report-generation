# C2 loss comparison: plain BCE vs √-weighted BCE vs focal vs ASL

_Generated by `scripts/write_loss_comparison_md.py` from the stored artifacts. No locked-test data was used._

**Question.** Can loss functions designed for class imbalance improve rare-finding discrimination in DenseNet121 on CheXpert without changing the data distribution?

## 1. Design and control

All four arms share one generated base config (`configs/classifier/c2/`). A test enforces that they differ **only** in `loss`, `experiment_name` and `output_dir`.

Held fixed:
- DenseNet121 with ImageNet initialisation
- the Phase 1 patient-level train/validation split (133,644 / 28,671 frontal images)
- the 6 blank-image exclusions
- 224×224 aspect-preserving preprocessing
- the same augmentation
- the same label order
- uncertain → masked (zero loss and gradient), blank → 0
- AdamW (lr 1e-4, weight decay 1e-4), batch 32, AMP fp16 training
- ReduceLROnPlateau (factor 0.1, patience 1)
- 8 epochs maximum, early-stopping patience 3, seed 42
- TF32 off
- the same canonical `fp32_strict` evaluation

Checkpoint selection, early stopping **and** the LR scheduler all monitor validation **macro AUPRC** in every arm.

**Not a controlled comparison: C1.** C1 is shown only as an external historical reference. It was selected by macro AUROC and trained with TF32 on, so C1-vs-C2 differences are not a controlled loss comparison.

**Reproducibility check.** C2-B is the same configuration as C1 apart from the selection metric. Its epochs 1–4 reproduce C1's training loss exactly to 4 decimals (0.5082, 0.4790, 0.4652, 0.4531). So training is deterministic across restarts and a reboot. C1's LR dropped after epoch 4; C2-B's dropped after epoch 6, which is why the later epochs diverge.

## 2. Loss formulas and configurations (fixed before any C2 result)

p = σ(z), z = logits. Every term is multiplied by the validity mask and divided by the number of valid entries.

| Arm | Formula | Parameters |
|---|---|---|
| C2-A BCE | −[y log p + (1−y) log(1−p)] | none |
| C2-B √-weighted BCE | −[w_c·y log p + (1−y) log(1−p)], w_c = √(N_neg,c / N_pos,c) | counts from the TRAIN split only (valid entries; blank counted as negative, uncertain excluded) |
| C2-C focal | −(1−p_t)^γ log p_t | γ = 2.0, no α, no class weights |
| C2-D ASL (Ridnik et al. 2021 defaults) | y·[−(1−p)^γ⁺ log p] + (1−y)·[−p_m^γ⁻ log(1−p_m)], p_m = max(p − m, 0) | γ⁺ = 0, γ⁻ = 4, m (clip) = 0.05 |

No ASL or focal parameter was tuned. The √ weighting for C2-B was chosen up front to match C1 and to avoid raw weights up to 74.8.

- **√-WBCE** trades small losses on Support Devices (-0.0004 AUPRC) and Pleural Effusion (-0.0022) for gains on Edema (+0.0101), Lung Opacity (+0.0082) and Atelectasis (+0.0055). There is no material common-class damage.
- **Micro metrics move the other way.** √-WBCE has the lowest micro AUROC (0.8922 vs BCE 0.9028) and micro AUPRC (0.6674 vs 0.7083). Micro metrics pool all (image, label) pairs, so they reward probabilities that are comparable **across** classes. Class weighting inflates rare-class probabilities relative to common ones, which hurts pooled ranking even though per-class discrimination improves.
- **Consequence for Phase 3:** per-class Youden thresholds are unaffected by this, but any use of raw probabilities across classes (for example ranking findings against each other) is.

Per-epoch tables are in each arm's `ANALYSIS.md` and `training_history.csv`. Curves: `figures/learning_curves_metrics.png`, `figures/learning_curves_loss.png`. Loss values are not comparable between loss functions, only the curve shapes are.

**Stability.** No arm showed NaN/Inf, gradient explosion or OOM. Maximum gradient norm per arm (always epoch 1): BCE 2.76, WBCE 4.78, Focal 1.91, ASL 1.33; after epoch 1 every arm stays below 3.2. Gradient scales differ between losses because the loss scales differ. A few AMP loss-scale skips (at most 3 per epoch) are normal fp16 calibration. All arms ran the full 8 epochs, so none stopped early.

**Overfitting.** Mild in every arm: training loss falls monotonically, while validation loss reaches its minimum early (epoch 2–5) and then drifts up. Validation macro AUPRC plateaus at about 0.41–0.42 from epoch 5.

**Underfitting.** None evident. But ASL's best epoch is the last (8), so it may still have been improving at the 8-epoch cap.

**Scheduler confound.** The scheduler follows each arm's own validation metric, so the arms did not receive the same LR schedule. √-WBCE's LR dropped after epoch 6 and ASL's after epoch 7; BCE's and focal's never dropped. √-WBCE's best epoch (7) and ASL's best epoch (8) come right after those drops. Part of √-WBCE's lead over BCE may therefore reflect the LR decay rather than the loss. A confirmation run should fix the LR schedule to remove this.

**Timing.** Peak VRAM is 2.28 GB for every arm. Steady-state epochs (2–8) averaged: BCE 14.2 min, WBCE 16.6 min, Focal 13.1 min, ASL 13.2 min training plus about 1.7–2 min validation. Total wall-clock (§3) is inflated for BCE and √-WBCE by 55–65 min cold-disk first epochs, and the machine was interrupted during the study, so timing differences between arms are not attributable to the loss function and were not tested.

**Run integrity.** Three runs were interrupted and restarted from scratch with identical configs: C2-A (stopped at the user's request), C2-B (the process died silently in epoch 7) and C2-C (killed by a laptop hard reset at 20:03). The partial outputs are kept under `_interrupted_*` and never used. Training is deterministic, so the completed runs match what an uninterrupted run would give (verified on C2-B vs C1, §1).

## 8. Decision

Rule applied (from the task): rank on (1) macro AUPRC, (2) per-class AUPRC, (3) rare-class improvement, (4) common-class regression, (5) macro AUROC, (6) stability, (7) magnitude of improvement over baseline.

| Loss | Macro AUPRC | Δ vs BCE [95% CI] | Rare-6 mean AUPRC | Common-5 mean AUPRC | Macro AUROC | Verdict |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| BCE | 0.4171 | n/a | 0.1490 | 0.6869 | 0.7989 | control |
| WBCE | 0.4235 | +0.0064 [+0.0031, +0.0096] | 0.1562 | 0.6912 | 0.8011 | **lead candidate** |
| Focal | 0.4190 | +0.0019 [-0.0006, +0.0046] | 0.1504 | 0.6869 | 0.7988 | **second candidate** |
| ASL | 0.4150 | -0.0020 [-0.0048, +0.0007] | 0.1466 | 0.6829 | 0.7949 | **not promoted** (as configured) |

**Recommendation: carry two losses forward, √-weighted BCE and focal (γ = 2), to multi-seed confirmation, with plain BCE as the control.**

- **√-weighted BCE (lead).**
  - It has the highest macro AUPRC (0.4235; +0.0064 over BCE, CI excludes 0).
  - It improves AUPRC in all six rare classes, with the best result on 8 of 14 classes by AUPRC and AUROC.
  - Macro AUROC is up only +0.0022 (CI [-0.0001, +0.0043]), so the gain is modest and the AUROC part is not established.
  - Its costs are the lowest micro metrics (cross-class probabilities are less comparable) and the scheduler confound in §7.
- **Focal.**
  - It is a close second on macro AUPRC (0.4190). Against BCE the difference is +0.0019, CI [-0.0006, +0.0046], so it is not distinguishable from BCE here.
  - It is stable (no instability in training) and it preserves cross-class probabilities (its micro metrics match BCE).
  - It deserves a second look because the √-WBCE result may be partly scheduler-driven. A multi-seed run decides whether focal's small edge is real.
- **ASL, as configured (γ⁺ 0, γ⁻ 4, clip 0.05).** It is lowest on macro AUROC and macro AUPRC (0.4150); its AUROC deficit against BCE is -0.0040 (CI excludes 0). It is worst on Fracture and Cardiomegaly. This is a statement about one fixed configuration within an 8-epoch budget, not about ASL in general: its best epoch was the last one.
- **Plain BCE** is competitive: within 0.006 macro AUPRC of the best arm, and best on micro AUROC and Support Devices. The gains from the imbalance losses are small.

**Next experiment (needs your approval, not started).**
- Multi-seed (for example 3 seeds) of √-WBCE, focal and the BCE control.
- Fix the LR schedule identically across arms, to remove the scheduler confound.
- Report patient-bootstrap CIs plus across-seed variation.
- Threshold optimisation stays separate and comes after the loss choice.

## 9. Limitations

- One seed per arm, so there is no training-variance estimate.
- **Epoch-to-epoch noise is comparable to the gaps between arms.** The median absolute epoch-to-epoch change in validation macro AUPRC (epochs 2–8) is BCE 0.0041, WBCE 0.0028, Focal 0.0047, ASL 0.0060, against between-arm gaps of 0.002–0.008. Taking the best of 8 epochs also gives each arm a selection-optimistic number (best epoch vs the following epoch: BCE 0.4171 vs 0.4119, WBCE 0.4235 vs 0.4196, Focal 0.4190 vs 0.4098, ASL 0.4150 vs n/a (best was the last epoch)). The ranking is therefore a screening signal, not a verdict.
- The scheduler confound described in §7.
- Labels come from an automatic report labeler with blank scored as negative, so false-negative noise affects every class.
- Validation-set metrics only. The locked test split was never opened.
- ASL was evaluated at one standard configuration only, with no tuning by design.
