# Methods: operating-policy selection (C4)

## Threshold policies

Operating points were derived from the sigmoid output scores of the selected DenseNet-121 classifier (14 independent outputs, one per CheXpert observation; √-weighted binary cross-entropy training). Scores were not normalised across classes, and they are treated as model scores rather than calibrated probabilities. A finding was called positive when its score was greater than or equal to a class-specific threshold. Four policies were compared: (i) a fixed threshold of 0.50 for every class; (ii) Youden's J thresholds, which maximise sensitivity + specificity − 1 for each class; (iii) F1-optimal thresholds, which maximise the F1 score for each class; and (iv) the F1-optimal thresholds combined with a No Finding consistency rule. Candidate thresholds were the distinct validation scores of each class. Exact ties were resolved deterministically (Youden: higher sensitivity, then higher threshold; F1: higher recall, then higher threshold).

## No Finding consistency rule

Because No Finding is not logically compatible with a pathological finding, the rule set the final No Finding output to negative whenever at least one of the twelve pathology observations (all observations except No Finding and Support Devices) was predicted positive; otherwise No Finding remained positive only if its own score exceeded its threshold. Support Devices was excluded from the rule because the reference labels permit No Finding together with a device (1,126 of 2,538 validation No Finding images also had Support Devices positive). The rule altered only the final binary No Finding output; scores and all other predictions were unchanged.

## Evaluation procedure

All analyses used the 28,671 frontal images of the 9,530 patients in the validation partition, with predictions generated once under a fixed single-precision evaluation policy and reused without further inference. Uncertain reference labels were excluded from every metric and blank labels were treated as negative. For each class and policy we computed precision, recall, specificity, F1 and balanced accuracy from the confusion counts, and report unweighted macro averages over all 14 observations and over the 13 observations other than No Finding. Undefined values were not replaced by zero. The number of positive findings per image was computed over the 13 observations other than No Finding and compared with the number of positive reference labels. Rare-class behaviour was examined for six classes defined in advance in the loss-comparison study, using false positives per true positive. The No Finding contradiction rate was defined as the percentage of images with No Finding and at least one pathology observation both positive. Before the analysis, the previously reported threshold-dependent metrics were reproduced from the stored scores and thresholds.

## Policy selection and test-set handling

Policies were compared qualitatively on macro F1, precision, recall, rare-class false-alarm burden, findings per image and logical consistency, without a composite score. Thresholds and the final policy were selected using the validation partition only. The held-out test partition was not used at any stage of threshold or policy selection and remained unevaluated, so that it can serve as an untouched final evaluation once the complete classifier configuration, including the loss function and score calibration, has been fixed. Threshold-dependent results reported here therefore describe validation operating-point performance and are optimistic by construction.
