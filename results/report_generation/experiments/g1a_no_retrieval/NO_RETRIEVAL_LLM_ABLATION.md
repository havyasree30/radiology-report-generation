# G1A: no-retrieval LLM ablation

_Generated by `scripts/g1a_06_write_docs.py` from the G1A artifacts. Retrieval-validation studies only; the locked retrieval-test split was not opened; classifier, retrieval configuration, finding extractor and populations are those of G1._

> **Read first.** **Degenerate G1A behaviour (post hoc diagnostic; the prompt was not changed).** For 240 of the 261 studies in which the classifier output was normal (92.0%), G1A wrote an enlarged-cardiomediastinum report that no classifier output supported: the model reproduced the first term of the prompt's standard-term list when given 'No Finding: positive'. 45.3% of all G1A reports are one identical text, versus 36.4% for G1 (whose most common text is the normal report); 6 G1A reports contain leaked reasoning tokens and 5 stopped at the token limit (G1: 0 and 0). The G1 − G1A contrast therefore reflects the robustness of this prompt/model combination without retrieval context, and the removed retrieval-linked rule, in addition to retrieved text itself, and it must not be read as a pure effect of retrieved reports. Within the 178 classifier-abnormal studies (supplementary stratified analysis, where G1A is not degenerate), G1 minus G1A was: precision +0.092 (95% CI +0.034 to +0.150), recall +0.149 (95% CI +0.079 to +0.217), F1 +0.119 (95% CI +0.060 to +0.175), classifier FP propagation -0.156 (95% CI -0.253 to -0.064), TP retention +0.154 (95% CI +0.043 to +0.250). Within classifier-normal studies the hallucination-rate difference was -0.920 (95% CI -0.960 to -0.881), driven by the degenerate output described above.

## 1. Question and design

What does retrieval add when the classifier and the language model are held constant? G1A uses the same local model, decoding, classifier output, studies, report format, finding extractor and metrics as G1; the only experimental difference is that G1A receives no retrieved reports, no retrieval scores and no evidence-support counts. Generator check: Ollama 0.35.1, `medgemma1.5:4b` digest `433252621ab1`, Q4_K_M; identical to G1 on version, tag, digest, quantization, generation options, context and output limits (all checks passed). Options: temperature 0.0, top_k 1, seed 42, 8192-token context, 400 new tokens at most.

## 2. Inputs and prompt

G1A receives only the frozen classifier-positive findings, their calibrated probabilities and the No Finding state. The classifier block of the user message is byte-identical to G1's for 547 of 547 studies; 0 messages contain retrieval wording, and 0 contain a reference sentence. The G1A system prompt is the G1 prompt with exactly the retrieval-dependent wording removed (the clause about retrieved excerpts; the rule on retrieved support; the rule on treating classifier positives without retrieved support cautiously; the retrieval mentions in the remaining rules); `g1a_prompt_diff_vs_g1.txt` lists every change. Prompt version `g1a-no-retrieval-v1`, SHA-256 `e5a4665af2a1d24062a45f7fe3dde9bbc03cad235fa858788fdc6da2ab686b88`, frozen before bulk generation (G1 prompt `476b05326f85…`). Because the G1 rule on treating classifier-positive findings without retrieved support cautiously cannot exist without retrieval, the G1 − G1A contrast measures the retrieval context together with that retrieval-linked instruction. Empty-classifier studies keep the G1 behaviour (rule 9 of the G1A prompt, the same classifier block).

Smoke test (4 studies, implementation checks only): passed, 0 format deviations; the prompt was not revised. Bulk generation: 547 of 547 reports for the same 547 studies as G1, 0 failures, 98.9% format-compliant, 31 minutes.

## 9. Copying

Corpus-wide (all IU corpus reports; sentences of at least six words): G1A copies 4.0% of its long sentences verbatim from some corpus report and G1 47.0%; of long sentences, 4.0% (G1A) and 33.0% (G1) are generic template sentences occurring in at least 10 corpus reports. Whole reports whose long sentences all occur in the corpus: G1A 3.5%, G1 22.7%; exact duplicates of an entire corpus report: G1A 0.0%, G1 10.2%. With the G1 definition against the study's own Top-5 retrieved texts, the copied-sentence rate is 0.0% for G1A (which never saw them: the control level) and 26.8% for G1; whole-report copies 0.0% versus 21.9%. Mean report length: G1A 15.1 words, G1 23.9. Short generic sentences recur in IU reports, so copying is interpreted relative to the G1A control and not as plagiarism.

## 10. Limitations

- One 4-billion-parameter local model, one frozen prompt per system, greedy decoding, one run; no replicate generations; Ollama does not guarantee bitwise GPU determinism.
- The G1 − G1A contrast includes the retrieval-linked instruction that had to be removed from the G1A prompt; it is the effect of the retrieval context as G1 implemented it, not of retrieved text in isolation.
- Finding extraction is rule-based and the reference is MeSH-derived and incomplete; per-finding counts are small, especially for rare findings, and Enlarged Cardiomediastinum has no reference positives.
- Normal studies dominate the clinical subset; the normal/abnormal analysis is descriptive.
- Validation data only; selection of nothing was made on these results; the locked retrieval test remains unopened; G2 has not been started.

## 11. Conclusion

With classifier, model and decoding held constant, the retrieval context changed the main finding metrics: precision was higher (favourable), recall was higher (favourable), F1 was higher (favourable), hallucination rate was lower (favourable), omission rate did not differ reliably, classifier FP propagation was lower (favourable), TP retention was higher (favourable), abnormal recall was lower (unfavourable). Retrieval added 136 and removed 306 findings, with a net +33 reference-matching and -203 unsupported findings. These differences are confounded by the degenerate G1A behaviour on classifier-normal studies described at the top of this document. Over-normalisation is present in G1A and is larger with the retrieved context: the controlled comparison supports a contribution of the retrieval context in addition to the language-model/prompt behaviour. This reading is tentative: G1A abnormal recall is inflated by its unsupported abnormal reports for classifier-normal studies, and the share of normal reports given an abnormal classifier output did not differ reliably between G1A and G1. G1 minus G1A retention is -0.048 (95% CI -0.300 to +0.211) for rare and +0.198 (95% CI +0.090 to +0.302) for other findings; the rare-minus-other retention gap differs between G1 and G1A by -0.246 (95% CI -0.496 to +0.025), so the data do not show that retrieval disproportionately suppresses rare findings (small counts limit this conclusion). No change was made to the classifier, the generator model or the retrieval configuration.
